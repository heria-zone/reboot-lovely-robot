# CONCEPT.md - Guidelines

## Purpose
The CONCEPT.md document serves as the creative foundation of your project. It captures raw ideas, inspirations, and initial thoughts without the constraints of formal structure. This document is your "idea sanctuary" where creativity flows freely and serves as a reference point throughout the project lifecycle to ensure you don't lose sight of your original vision.

## When to Create
- At the very beginning of the ideation process
- Before any formal planning or design work
- When you have a spark of inspiration that needs to be captured
- When brainstorming new features for existing projects

## How to Approach

### 1. Set Aside Judgment
- Write without self-editing or criticism
- Don't worry about feasibility or constraints at this stage
- Capture everything, even seemingly tangential ideas

### 2. Use a Stream of Consciousness Approach
- Write as ideas come to you
- Don't force a structure initially
- Use bullet points, diagrams, or whatever format works for your thought process
- Record voice notes and transcribe them if that helps capture ideas more naturally

### 3. Focus on the "Why" and "What" (Not the "How")
- Emphasize the problems you're trying to solve
- Describe the vision of what success looks like
- Discuss who would benefit and how
- Avoid getting caught in implementation details too early

### 4. Include Real-World Scenarios
- Describe how you envision people using the solution
- Provide specific examples and use cases
- Tell stories that illustrate the need and impact

### 5. Capture Inspiration Sources
- Note where ideas came from (articles, conversations, other products, etc.)
- Record why these inspirations resonated with you
- Collect reference materials, links, screenshots

### 6. Document Questions and Uncertainties
- Write down aspects you're unsure about
- List assumptions that need validation
- Note areas where you need more information or expertise

### 7. Revisit and Expand
- Return to this document regularly to add new thoughts
- Date new additions to track the evolution of ideas
- Don't delete earlier ideas—strike through outdated concepts but keep them visible

## Recommended Sections

1. **Vision Statement**
   - One paragraph describing the core idea
   - The fundamental purpose and value proposition

2. **Problem Space**
   - What issues are you addressing?
   - Who experiences these problems?
   - Why existing solutions are inadequate

3. **Free-form Ideation**
   - Unconstrained brainstorming
   - All ideas, features, approaches you can think of
   - Potential alternatives and variations

4. **Use Case Scenarios**
   - Examples of how it would work in practice
   - "A day in the life" with your solution
   - Different user types and their experiences

5. **Inspiration Sources**
   - Products, articles, or experiences that inspired this idea
   - How your concept builds upon or differs from these sources

6. **Questions & Uncertainties**
   - Things you're still figuring out
   - Areas needing research or validation
   - Potential roadblocks or challenges

7. **Success Criteria**
   - How you'll know if the idea succeeds
   - Qualitative and quantitative indicators
   - Impact you hope to achieve

8. **Parking Lot**
   - Related ideas that might be explored later
   - Alternative approaches to consider
   - "Nice to have" features

## Tips for Effectiveness

- **Use Visual Aids**: Sketch, diagram, or create simple wireframes to help visualize concepts
- **Stay Media-Rich**: Include links, images, and references that support your thinking
- **Remove Constraints**: Don't limit yourself by technology, budget, or timeline at this stage
- **Be Messy**: This document shouldn't look polished—embrace the chaos of creativity
- **Date Your Entries**: Track when ideas emerged and evolved
- **Avoid Premature Solution**: Focus on defining the problem thoroughly before jumping to solutions
- **Think Big**: Consider the ideal state without compromise, then work backward later
- **Record Emotional Reactions**: Note your excitement, concerns, or other feelings about aspects of the idea

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Premature Structure**: Forcing too much organization too early can stifle creativity
- **Implementation Focus**: Getting caught up in "how" rather than "what" and "why"
- **Excessive Constraints**: Limiting ideas based on perceived feasibility too early
- **Single Perspective**: Considering only your viewpoint rather than various stakeholders
- **Solution Fixation**: Becoming attached to a specific solution before fully exploring the problem
- **Perfectionism**: Striving for a polished document rather than capturing raw thoughts
- **Under-Documentation**: Not capturing enough detail to recall your full thinking later

## Relationship to Other Documents

This CONCEPT.md serves as the foundation for all other project documents:
- **DESIGN.md** will formalize and structure these raw ideas
- **PLANNING.md** will determine how to technically implement the vision
- **ARCHITECTURE.md** will detail the components needed to realize the concept
- **TASK.md** will break down the work required to build it
- **README.md** will introduce others to the project based on this concept

Always refer back to this document throughout the project lifecycle to ensure you're staying true to the original vision and purpose.
