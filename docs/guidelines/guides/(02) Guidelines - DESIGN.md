# DESIGN.md - Guidelines

## Purpose
The DESIGN.md document transforms your raw concepts into a structured, accessible presentation for both technical and non-technical stakeholders. It formalizes your vision, creates clarity around what you aim to build, and serves as the bridge between initial ideas and technical implementation. This document should be approachable enough for non-developers while containing sufficient detail to guide the development team.

## When to Create
- After capturing initial ideas in CONCEPT.md
- Before diving into technical planning and architecture
- When you need to communicate your vision to stakeholders
- When seeking feedback on the overall approach
- Before committing significant resources to implementation

## How to Approach

### 1. Review and Refine Your Concept Document
- Extract the core ideas and value propositions
- Identify patterns and common themes
- Prioritize features and capabilities
- Eliminate contradictions and resolve uncertainties where possible

### 2. Define Clear Scope Boundaries
- Determine what is in scope for the initial version
- Explicitly state what is out of scope
- Create a distinction between "must have" and "nice to have" features
- Define minimum viable product (MVP) criteria

### 3. Adopt a User-Centered Perspective
- Define your target users or audience precisely
- Create user personas if helpful
- Organize features around user needs and journeys
- Prioritize based on user impact and value

### 4. Create a Logical Structure
- Organize information hierarchically
- Group related features and capabilities
- Ensure a natural progression of ideas
- Use consistent terminology throughout

### 5. Visualize Key Concepts
- Create wireframes or mockups of user interfaces
- Develop flow diagrams for key processes
- Use visual aids to communicate complex ideas
- Include conceptual models and diagrams

### 6. Validate With Stakeholders
- Share early drafts with key stakeholders
- Incorporate feedback into revisions
- Confirm that the design addresses the core needs
- Ensure alignment on priorities and approach

### 7. Plan for Evolution
- Acknowledge areas that might change or need refinement
- Identify future expansions or enhancements
- Define criteria for when to revisit design decisions
- Create a framework for feedback collection

## Recommended Sections

1. **Executive Summary**
   - Concise overview of the project purpose and value
   - Key differentiators and innovations
   - Target audience and primary use cases
   - Expected outcomes and benefits

2. **Problem Statement**
   - Clear articulation of the problem being solved
   - Impact and significance of the problem
   - Current approaches and their limitations
   - Opportunity for improvement

3. **User Stories**
   - "As a [user type], I want [feature/capability] so that [benefit]"
   - Prioritized list of user needs
   - Acceptance criteria for each story
   - User journey maps for primary scenarios

4. **Key Features and Capabilities**
   - Detailed descriptions of main functionality
   - Prioritization (must-have, should-have, could-have, won't-have)
   - Relationships between features
   - Unique selling points and differentiators

5. **User Experience**
   - Overall approach to user experience
   - Key user flows and journeys
   - Wireframes or mockups of critical interfaces
   - Interaction patterns and principles

6. **Constraints and Requirements**
   - Technical limitations or requirements
   - Business constraints (time, budget, resources)
   - Regulatory or compliance considerations
   - Performance, security, and reliability expectations

7. **Success Metrics**
   - Quantifiable measures of success
   - Key performance indicators (KPIs)
   - Evaluation methodology
   - Baseline measurements and targets

8. **Timeline Overview**
   - Major phases and milestones
   - Dependencies and critical path items
   - Release strategy (phased, all-at-once, etc.)
   - Key decision points

9. **Glossary**
   - Definitions of key terms and concepts
   - Consistent terminology for technical and domain-specific language
   - Abbreviations and acronyms explained

10. **Stakeholder Analysis**
    - Key stakeholders and their interests
    - Communication needs and frequency
    - Decision-making authority
    - Success criteria for different stakeholder groups

## Tips for Effectiveness

- **Balance Detail and Clarity**: Provide enough detail to guide implementation without overwhelming readers
- **Use Consistent Formatting**: Establish and maintain consistent styling, headers, and notation
- **Create a Traceability Matrix**: Link design elements back to concepts and forward to technical implementation
- **Use Plain Language**: Avoid jargon and technical terms where possible, or explain them when necessary
- **Provide Context**: Explain why certain decisions were made, not just what they are
- **Focus on Outcomes**: Emphasize the results and benefits, not just features and functions
- **Separate Concerns**: Distinguish between user needs, business requirements, and technical constraints
- **Include Examples**: Provide concrete examples to illustrate abstract concepts
- **Maintain Readability**: Use white space, visuals, and formatting to enhance comprehension
- **Version and Date**: Track changes and evolution of the design document

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Feature Overload**: Including too many features without clear prioritization
- **Solution Before Problem**: Focusing on the solution before fully articulating the problem
- **Technical Jargon**: Using language that excludes non-technical stakeholders
- **Ambiguity**: Leaving room for multiple interpretations of requirements
- **Ignoring Constraints**: Designing without consideration for technical or business limitations
- **Over-specification**: Prescribing implementation details that should be left to technical planning
- **Missing User Perspective**: Focusing on technical or business concerns without centering user needs
- **Lack of Visual Aids**: Relying solely on text to communicate complex concepts
- **Inconsistent Terminology**: Using different terms for the same concept or the same term for different concepts
- **Scope Creep**: Continuously expanding the design scope without reprioritization

## Relationship to Other Documents

- **Builds Upon**: CONCEPT.md (your raw ideas and original vision)
- **Informs**: PLANNING.md (technical strategy and implementation approach)
- **Guides**: ARCHITECTURE.md (component design and data flows)
- **Directs**: TASK.md (work breakdown and priorities)
- **Summarized In**: README.md (project introduction and overview)

Regularly revisit this document throughout development to ensure implementation aligns with the design intent. Update as needed when significant changes occur, always maintaining its role as the definitive reference for what you are building and why.
